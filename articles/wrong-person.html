Climate Tech Keeps Solving the Wrong Person's Problem

Most sustainability apps are built for people who can already afford to be sustainable. The bigger opportunity — and the bigger impact — is everyone else.

September 2026 · 5 min read

The Problem

Open almost any sustainability app and the assumptions become obvious fast: you have a smartphone with reliable data, disposable income for the "eco" version of a product, a car worth calculating emissions for, a lifestyle with enough slack to add a new habit. The entire category has quietly agreed on a customer — someone already comfortable enough that sustainability is a lifestyle choice rather than a daily condition.

That customer is real, but small. And building almost exclusively for them means the industry has spent a decade optimizing nudges for people whose baseline footprint was never the problem, while the much larger population whose daily life is already low-carbon — not by choice, but by circumstance — gets nothing built for them at all.

A household that can't afford a car, buys unpackaged food because packaged costs more, and repairs instead of replacing isn't living outside the sustainability conversation. It's living the outcome that conversation claims to want. It just never gets counted, credited, or designed for, because it doesn't fit the customer profile the industry built around.

The people already living low-carbon lives were never the target market. They should have been the starting point.

This isn't just an equity problem. It's a missed-opportunity problem. The largest pool of real, existing environmental impact sits with people current sustainability products were never built to reach — not because their impact is small, but because they don't look like the user persona in the deck.

How Ecoverse Approaches It

Ecoverse isn't designed around the assumption of a smartphone-first, disposable-income user with room in their life for a new app habit. It's built hyperlocal and language-first specifically so it can reach the people already generating the impact the rest of the industry is chasing — through local data, community verification, and channels that don't require the user to look a particular way first.

The goal isn't to convince people who are already living low-carbon lives to do something new. It's to finally build the infrastructure that notices what they're already doing, verifies it, and lets it stand as real, valuable impact — on equal footing with anything a more resourced user could generate.

Design for the person circumstance already made sustainable, and the addressable population for real impact stops being a niche. It becomes almost everyone.

Where This Leads

Built for who's actually doing it.

The people already living sustainably were never the edge case. Ecoverse is built to start with them, not get to them eventually.
